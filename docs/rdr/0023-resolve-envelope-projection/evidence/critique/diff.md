## 4. CONTRADICTIONS

**None.** No claim in either pass is incompatible with a claim in the other about the same passage. The two closest calls are differences of emphasis, not of fact:

1. **`0023:§source-authority-census`, "All fifteen are written at one assembly site."** A (C-1) treats this sentence as a defect — three counts of one set (fifteen prose / thirteen rows / fourteen struct fields). B (C-2) quotes the *same sentence* approvingly as a true statement of single-site-ness, using it to argue the "single-site edit" framing understates the pointer migration's blast radius. These are not contradictory: B's use depends only on the single-writer half, which A explicitly verified as true ("the single-writer half is true and I verified it… The count is wrong"). B simply did not audit the count. Settled by: `rg` for `resolvePayload{` in `internal/cli` (one literal, `flow_resolve.go:223`) plus `reflect.TypeOf(resolvePayload{}).NumField()` — confirms one writer *and* fourteen fields, so both readings hold on their own terms.

2. **`0023:C2`'s treatment of `model` and `revision`.** A (C-5) argues `revision` should not be in the projected width at all (dead weight in the mode built to remove weight). B (C-5) argues `model` should be *added* to the always-keep core because `revision` cannot substitute for it. These point in opposite remediation directions but make compatible factual claims — both rest on `revision` being constant-empty, which is verified at `internal/cli/flow_exec.go:49`. The disagreement is about the fix, not about the passage's state, so it is not a contradiction; it is a genuine open design fork for the fix half to resolve (drop `revision`, promote `model`, or activate `revision`).

## 5. DISAGREEMENT ANALYSIS

**Where A went deeper.** Most of A-only is depth, not divergence: A ran verifications B did not attempt. A counted the census rows against `reflect` (A:C-1), read 0024's *implementation plan* rather than only its contract and found two extensional 0010 oracles that break in either landing order (A:C-2), traced `invokedReaders`'s call site to establish the reader oracle is a tautology (A:C-6), and reproduced the pointer conversion far enough to observe `"readers":null` (A:C-15). B stopped at the RDR prose plus a struct-type read for each of these anchors. The strongest example is `0023:A4`: both passes read the joint-decision material, but only A followed the citation into the sibling's implementation plan — B instead audited the *breadth* of the sweep (B:C-8, the unlisted 0018) and missed the depth defect in the one collision that did fire. A also carries a class of structural findings B did not look for at all: oracle self-comparison, missing pre-change golden (A:C-16), phase-ordering CI break (A:C-17), proportionality (A:C-10, A:C-19).

**Where B went deeper.** Two B-only rows are genuine depth A missed. B:C-2 is the sharper read of the A2 mechanism: A noted the nil-vs-empty hazard but B saw the *Go-level* consequences — composite-literal addressability forcing new locals, and A3's Verified claim covering `NumField` but not `reflect.Kind`, leaving an uninventoried class of tests. A:C-15 and B:C-2 touch the same passage from different distances and do not overlap in substance. B:C-7 is the other: A dismissed the whole-tree walker as over-specified ceremony guarding a non-defect, while B took the walker seriously enough to notice it is coupled to cobra's private lazy-init path and will silently go vacuous on a version bump. That is a real forward-looking failure A's proportionality argument talked itself out of finding.

**Genuine divergence of reading.** Only one, and it is on `0023:C1`'s whole-tree walker. A reads it as disproportionate ceremony whose blast radius is one unusable flag on `completion` (A:C-10) and would cut it; B reads it as the RDR's most structurally novel and highest-risk piece, the schedule's long pole, and would *strengthen* it with a pre-lock spike (B:C-1, B:C-7). Both agree A5 is improperly Pending (convergent M-2) — they disagree about whether the clause it supports deserves to exist. That fork, and the `model`-vs-`revision` fork in §4.2, are the two places the fix half must choose rather than merge.
